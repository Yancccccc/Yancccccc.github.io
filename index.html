01

Abstract

Multi-view spacecraft reconstruction from images acquired around a target can provide important geometric information for on-orbit inspection. Weakly textured regions, however, make cross-view matching unreliable and can lead to incomplete surfaces. Per-view depth estimation and completion provide dense geometry, but local inconsistencies may place the same surface at different 3D positions. We address this problem by validating scale-aligned depth observations across views, transferring reliable local surface attributes to structured 2D Gaussians, and reducing the influence of incompatible depth observations during self-supervised optimization. Experiments on MVSPAD show improved cross-view depth consistency and surface reconstruction accuracy.

02

Method

The framework turns independently recovered per-view depth maps into a shared surface through three connected stages. Reliable observations are retained, their local geometry is preserved, and inconsistent evidence is weakened as the surface is optimized.

M1

Multi-View Depth Validation

Sample local hypotheses along reference rays and test them using depth consistency, effective support, and viewing directions.

Output: validated surface seeds
→
M2

Structured 2D Gaussian Initialization

Map seed position, normal, tangent scale, color, and confidence to surface-aligned 2D Gaussian parameters.

Output: trainable surface primitives
→
M3

Conflict-Aware Surface Optimization

Retain compatible depth constraints, weaken conflicting observations, and refine the surface with RGB and multi-view geometry.

✓×
Output: optimized depth and mesh
